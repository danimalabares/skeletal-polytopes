## Primary sources for the definitions

**[BJS]** A. Breda D'Azevedo, G. A. Jones, E. Schulte,
*Constructions of chiral polytopes of small rank*,
Canadian Journal of Mathematics **63** (2011), 1254–1283.
arXiv:1008.1080 (<https://arxiv.org/abs/1008.1080>).

* §2 (arXiv pp. 3–8): regular and chiral polytopes; `σ_i := ρ_{i-1} ρ_i`
  (p. 4); the distinguished generators of a chiral polytope with the
  orientation convention `σ_i(F'_i) = F_i` (p. 4); relations
  `σ_i^{p_i} = (σ_i σ_{i+1} ⋯ σ_j)^2 = ε` (eq. (3), p. 4); rank-4 intersection
  condition (eq. (5), p. 5); the direct-regularity criterion "Γ admits an
  involutory automorphism mapping `σ_1,…,σ_{n-1}` to
  `σ_1^{-1}, σ_1^2 σ_2, σ_3, …, σ_{n-1}`, induced by conjugation with `ρ_0`"
  (pp. 5–6); definition of the enantiomorphic (mirror image) word `ω̄` (p. 6);
  Lemma 2.1 (`ω` and `ω̄` have the same period in a directly regular group);
  the mix `Δ ◊ Δ'` of two groups with specified generators as the subgroup
  of `Δ × Δ'` generated by the pairs `(α_j, α'_j)` (eq. (7), p. 7).
* §4 *Chirality groups* (arXiv pp. 9–12): `W = ⟨r_0,…,r_{n-1}⟩` the universal
  string Coxeter group, `W^+ = ⟨s_i = r_{i-1} r_i⟩` with presentation
  `(s_i s_{i+1} ⋯ s_j)^2 = ε` (eq. (11), p. 10); `Γ^+(P) = W^+/M`;
  `M^{r_0} := r_0 M r_0^{-1}`; `M_W := M ∩ M^{r_0}`, `M^W := M M^{r_0}`
  (diagram (12), p. 10); Lemma 4.1 (`M^W/M ≅ M/M_W ≅ M^W/M^{r_0} ≅ M^{r_0}/M_W`);
  the **definition of the chirality group** `X(P)` as this common quotient and
  of the chirality index `κ(P) = |X(P)|` (p. 11); `X(P) = M^W/M ⊴ Γ^+(P)` with
  `Γ^+(P)/X(P) = W^+/M^W` (eq. (13)); *totally chiral* means `X(P) = Γ^+(P)`
  (p. 11); Lemma 4.2; the computational recipe "`X(P)` is the normal closure in
  `Γ(P)` of the mirrored relators `R^{r_0}`", with the explicit substitution
  `s_j ↦ (s_1 ⋯ s_{j-1})(s_1 ⋯ s_j)^{-1}`, i.e. `s_1^{-1}, s_1^2 s_2, s_j`
  (pp. 11–12).
* §5 *Mixing* (arXiv pp. 12–16): the mix `W⁺/M ◊ W⁺/K` of two rotation
  groups (eq. (15), p. 13) and `W⁺/(M ∩ K) ≅ W⁺/M ◊ W⁺/K` (eq. (16), p. 13);
  Lemma 5.5 (p. 14) and Remark 5.1 (p. 15) on chirality groups of mixes.

**[Cun]** G. Cunningham, *Mixing chiral polytopes*,
Journal of Algebraic Combinatorics **36** (2012), 263–277.
arXiv:1111.0701 (<https://arxiv.org/abs/1111.0701>).

* §2.3 (arXiv pp. 4–5): `σ_i` sends the base flag `Φ` to `Φ^{i,i-1}`; the
  direct-regularity criterion; the **enantiomorphic form** `P̄` (same face set,
  base flag `Φ^0`) with `Γ^+(P̄) = ⟨σ_1^{-1}, σ_1^2 σ_2, σ_3, …⟩`; the mirror
  word `w̄`; `Γ^+(P) = W^+/M` and `Γ^+(P̄) = W^+/M̄`.
* §3 (arXiv pp. 5–8): the mix `Γ ◊ Γ'` (p. 6); Proposition 3.1
  (`Γ^+(P ◊ Q) ≅ W^+/(M ∩ K)`); the comix and Proposition 3.2
  (`Γ^+(P) □ Γ^+(Q) ≅ W^+/MK`); Proposition 3.3
  (`|Γ^+(P ◊ Q)| · |Γ^+(P) □ Γ^+(Q)| = |Γ^+(P)| · |Γ^+(Q)|`), whose proof
  identifies `ker π_1 ≅ M/(M ∩ K)` for the projection `π_1` of the mix onto its
  first factor.
* §4 (arXiv pp. 8–12): `P ◊ P̄` is the minimal directly regular pre-polytope
  covering `P`, `P □ P̄` the maximal directly regular quotient; the natural maps
  `π_1 : Γ^+(P) ◊ Γ^+(P̄) → Γ^+(P)` and `π_2 : Γ^+(P) → Γ^+(P) □ Γ^+(P̄)` have
  isomorphic kernels, and this kernel is the chirality group `X(P)`;
  *totally chiral* (all p. 9).  Theorem 4.2 (p. 9), Proposition 4.5 (p. 10)
  (chirality criteria for mixes); §5 (p. 12): the chiral 5-polytope with
  group `S_6` has chirality group `A_6` ("a direct calculation in GAP").

**[SW]** E. Schulte, A. I. Weiss, *Chiral polytopes*, in: P. Gritzmann,
B. Sturmfels (eds.), *Applied Geometry and Discrete Mathematics — The Victor
Klee Festschrift*, DIMACS Series in Discrete Mathematics and Theoretical
Computer Science **4**, AMS/ACM, Providence RI, 1991, pp. 493–516.

* §2 (pp. 494–496): definitions; `ρ_i` fixes all but the `i`-face of `Φ`;
  rotations `σ_i := ρ_{i-1} ρ_i` (eq. (3)); relations (4)
  `σ_i^{p_i} = 1`, `(σ_i σ_{i+1} ⋯ σ_j)^2 = 1`; *directly regular*; chiral
  polytopes and the automorphisms `σ_i`.
* §3 (pp. 496–500): the orientation convention `σ_i(F'_i) = F_i` (p. 496);
  Lemma 1 (`τ_{i,j}(Φ) = (Φ^{i-1})^j`); Proposition 1 (two flag orbits: even
  and odd flags); Proposition 3 (`A(P) = ⟨σ_1,…,σ_{n-1}⟩` with relations (4));
  Proposition 6 (face stabilisers `⟨σ_2,…⟩`, `⟨σ_3, σ_1σ_2⟩`, `⟨σ_1, σ_2σ_3⟩`,
  `⟨σ_1,σ_2⟩` in rank 4); Proposition 7 (intersection property);
  Lemma 11 (p. 511): in rank 4 the intersection property (14) reduces to the
  three equalities used here (BJS eq. (5)).
* §5 (pp. 501 ff.): construction of a chiral or directly regular polytope from
  a group satisfying (10) and the intersection property (14); **Theorem 1**
  (p. 507, proof p. 509; the polytope is directly regular iff there is an
  involutory automorphism with `σ_1 ↦ σ_1^{-1}`, `σ_2 ↦ σ_1^2 σ_2`,
  `σ_j ↦ σ_j` for `j ≥ 3`; this is the criterion used by the thesis and by
  the legacy scripts).

  **Note.** [SW] supplies the regularity/chirality *criterion* (Theorem 1)
  and, in Theorem 1(c), the images `σ_1^{-1}, σ_1^2 σ_2, σ_j` that
  Cunningham later calls the generators of the enantiomorphic form (the word
  "enantiomorphic" does not occur in [SW]); it does **not** define the
  chirality group.  The chirality group was introduced for hypermaps by Breda
  D'Azevedo, Jones, Nedela and Škoviera (*Chirality groups of maps and
  hypermaps*, J. Algebraic Combin. **29** (2009), 337–355) and adapted to
  polytopes in [BJS, §4].

## The two polytopes

**[BHP]** J. Bracho, I. Hubard, D. Pellicer,
*A finite chiral 4-polytope in ℝ⁴*,
Discrete & Computational Geometry **52** (2014), 799–805.
DOI 10.1007/s00454-014-9631-4.

* §2 (pp. 800–803): the projective 4-polytope `Q` of type `{4,3,3}` built
  from a chiral colouring of `K_{4,4}`; **Theorem 1**: `Q` is combinatorially
  regular, isomorphic to the hemi-hypercube `{4,3,3}/2`, but geometrically
  chiral with geometrically chiral facets; its symmetry group has order 96.
* §3 (pp. 803–805): the double cover `Q̂ ⊂ S³ ⊂ ℝ⁴` — called "Roli's cube"
  in [Thesis] and in informal usage; BHP themselves write only `Q̂` — of
  Schläfli type `{8,3,3}`, with 4 facets `{8,3}` each having 16 vertices,
  24 edges and 6 faces; the generator of the stabiliser of a 2-face/3-face
  pair as a product of two 8-cycles on the 16 vertices; **Theorem 2**: `Q̂` is
  a chiral 4-polytope of full rank.

**[Thesis]** D. González-Casanova, *Two new chiral 4-polytopes in E⁴*,
master's thesis (tesina, Maestro en Ciencias), UNAM, October 2023
(director J. Bracho; I. Hubard thanked as co-tutor);
local file `~/github/tesina/tesina.tex`.  Paper version:
J. Bracho, D. González-Casanova, I. Hubard, *Two new chiral 4-polytopes of
full rank*, manuscript, this repository (`two-chiral.tex`, identical to the
legacy `my-masters-thesis.tex`).

* §3.1 *Regular and chiral abstract 4-polytopes*: `σ_i := ρ_{i-1} ρ_i`,
  relations (eq. `equation-chiral-rels`), intersection condition
  (eq. `equation-chiral-int`), face groups `Γ^0 = ⟨σ_2,σ_3⟩`,
  `Γ^1 = ⟨σ_3, σ_1σ_2⟩`, `Γ^2 = ⟨σ_1, σ_2σ_3⟩`, `Γ^3 = ⟨σ_1,σ_2⟩`, and the
  Schulte–Weiss criterion (eq. `equation-combinatorially-chiral`:
  `ρ(σ_1) = σ_1^{-1}`, `ρ(σ_2) = σ_1^2 σ_2`, `ρ(σ_3) = σ_3`).
* §3.2 *Realizations*: Wythoff's construction with base edge
  `e = v⟨S_1S_2⟩ = {v, vS_1^{-1}}`, base face `e⟨S_1⟩`, base cell
  `f⟨S_1,S_2⟩`.
* §4: `P_0 = R_0`, `P_1 = R_1R_2R_3R_2R_1R_0R_1R_2R_3R_2R_1`, `P_2 = R_3`,
  `P_3 = R_2` generate the group of the star polytope `{5/2,3,5}`.
* §5 *A chiral 4-polytope from {5/2,3,5}*: `S_1 = P_0P_1P_3P_2`,
  `S_2 = P_2P_1`, `S_3 = P_3P_2`; relations
  `S_1^{12} = S_2^3 = S_3^5 = (S_1S_2)^2 = (S_1S_2S_3)^2 = (S_2S_3)^2 = Id`;
  120 vertices, 720 edges, 300 faces, 50 cells; the name `{12/(1,5),3,5}`;
  the statement that no automorphism `ρ` as in the criterion exists.
