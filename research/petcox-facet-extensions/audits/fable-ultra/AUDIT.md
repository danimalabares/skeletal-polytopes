skeletal 4-polytope in `E^4` with geometrically chiral PETCOX cells, with the stated
types, f-vectors, group orders, distinguished generators, vertex figures and chirality

## 2. Priority 1–2: scope and completeness proof

### 2.1 What is actually classified (scope and normalisation)

The theorem the producer needs is the strongest of the three readings in the task:
*(3) all faithful geometrically chiral skeletal 4-polytopes in `E^4` whose cells are
congruent to some `H_alpha(T)`*.  theory.md §A reduces (3) to (1), *the Wythoff ansatz with
the PETCOX `S1,S2` fixed*.  The reduction is correct, but two steps are asserted rather than
proved (finding **F3**, minor):

* **Linearity of the third generator.**  `X = S3` is an isometry of `E^4`; the producer
  treats it as a matrix.  Proof: put the centre of the base cell at the origin, so `S1,S2`
  are linear.  Write `X: x ↦ xM + b`.  From `(S2X)^2 = 1` and `(S1S2X)^2 = 1` the affine
  map `A = S2X` satisfies `A^2 = 1`, `A S1 A = S1^{-1}`; comparing translation parts gives
  `bM = -b` and `b S1 M + b = 0`, hence `b S1 = b`.  Every `S1` is a twist without nonzero
  fixed vector on the effective 4-space (`misc-audit.g (e)`: fixed space of dimension 0,
  resp. 1 = the all-ones line in the 5-coordinate simplex model), so `b = 0`.  Thus
  `Γ = <S1,S2,X> ⊂ O(4)`.
* **Finiteness of `Γ` (also needed for E2, E4).**  The vertices of the base 2-face
  `w S1^k` span `E^4` (both components `w_1, w_2` are nonzero because the PETCOX circle
  avoids both invariant planes — verified for all ten families in `l3-audit.g`; the two
  rotation angles are distinct, non-opposite and different from `π`).  By discreteness the
  vertex set is finite, `Γ` acts on it, and an isometry fixing a spanning set is the
  identity, so `Γ` embeds in a finite symmetric group.  This also disposes of infinite
  (honeycomb-like) polytopes with PETCOX cells: none exist, so reading (3) includes them
  harmlessly.

With these two facts the rest of §A is sound: for a geometrically chiral `P`, `G(P)` acts
freely on flags with two orbits, is generated by the three rotations `S_i` of the base flag,
`P` is the Wythoff realisation of `P(G(P))`, the cell stabiliser is `<S1,S2>` = the
symmetry rotation group of the cell `≅ H_alpha(T)`, and choosing the base flag in the
PETCOX orbit (the other orbit gives the same polytope; a reflection gives the enantiomorph)
makes `S1,S2` literally the PETCOX matrices.  Base vertex on the circle `Fix(S2)` (B1–B2),
antipodal identification, and the exclusion of geometrically regular `P` (D1; 2-faces
spanning `E^4`, checked for all 24) are correct.  Hence **the ansatz is exhaustive** and the
classification is of type (3), not merely (1) or (2).

### 2.3 Attempted attacks that failed

* A `(5,5)` extension: none (above).  A `(4,4)`, `(4,5)` extension: impossible.
* An affine (non-linear) third generator: impossible (`b S1 = b ⇒ b = 0`).

## 6. Verdict

**Strongest theorem established by producer + audit:** Up to congruence and
enantiomorphism, the faithful geometrically chiral skeletal 4-polytopes in `E^4` whose
cells are PETCOX polyhedra `H_alpha(T)` are exactly the polytopes obtained from the 24
base vertices listed in RESULT.md; these form **6 abstract polytopes** (with the stated
types, f-vectors, group orders and chirality groups) and **12 realisations, two per
abstract class** (the 24 base vertices pair up into congruent polytopes).  In particular: Roli's cube has exactly two non-congruent chiral realisations with PETCOX cells (`alpha = 0` and `alpha = ∞`); class 2 has two (one on the 120-cell's vertices with `{5,3,3}` cells, one with `{5/2,3,3}` cells); the thesis class has two (the two thesis polytopes themselves); classes 4, 5, 6 have two each.  The existence of classes 4, 5, 6 and of the second realisation of class 2 is unaffected.

**Strongest theorem established by the producer's own material alone (without the
audit's supplements):** the 24 realisations exist and have the stated properties; the six
abstract classes are correct; every extension whose vertex-figure group is `A4`, `S4`, or an
`A5` generated by rotations of orders `(3,5)` or `(5,3)` is one of the 24; and the 24 are
pairwise non-congruent whenever their vertex sets differ in Gram multiset or lie on a common
`W`-invariant vertex set.

**Principal verdict:** **REFUTED** — the central statement "exactly 24 faithful geometrically chiral skeletal 4-polytopes in `E^4` with PETCOX cells, up to congruence and enantiomorphism, four per abstract class" is false: there are exactly 12, two per class, as certified by explicit similarities.  The supplied completeness proof also had a genuine gap (F1), closed here.  What survives, and is now established (producer's material plus this audit's supplements), is the corrected classification stated above: six abstract polytopes, twelve realisations, complete.
